Color Identification from Photo

Whitefield

A visual utility for extracting useful colors from real-world images.

Overview

Whitefield is built around a simple but practical design problem: useful colors are everywhere, but translating them from a photo into a usable palette is often more awkward than it should be.

The project explores a cleaner, more approachable way to identify colors, understand their relationships, and turn them into something a designer or builder can actually use.

What the product is trying to solve

Many color tools are either too technical for casual use or too shallow for meaningful work. Whitefield aims to sit in the middle: simple enough to feel immediate, but structured enough to be useful.

It treats a photo not just as an image, but as a source of visual information that can be extracted, organized, and turned into design input.

Core ideas

  • Color extraction that feels approachable and readable.
  • Visual analysis presented in a calm, uncluttered way.
  • Useful palette output rather than raw data alone.
  • A utility experience shaped by design thinking, not just function.

My role

Whitefield reflects my interest in turning visually rich ideas into practical tools. I shaped the product framing, naming, interaction approach, and interface direction, and used AI-assisted development to help bring the product into working form.
