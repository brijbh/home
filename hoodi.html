Hoodi

A workspace for writing, testing, comparing, and improving prompts.

Overview

Hoodi is a prompt operations workspace designed to bring more structure to prompt writing and evaluation. The idea came from a simple need: prompts often start as rough experiments, then quickly multiply into versions, edits, tests, notes, model comparisons, and revisions. What begins as one prompt can turn into an entire working system.

Hoodi explores how that system can be made clearer. Instead of treating prompts as throwaway chat inputs, it treats them as working assets that can be organized, refined, compared, and understood over time.

What the product is trying to solve

Prompt work is often messy. People copy and paste between tools, lose track of versions, and struggle to compare outputs in a meaningful way. Even when the result is useful, the process behind it can be hard to reproduce or improve.

Hoodi is built around the idea that better prompt workflows need more than a text box. They need structure, visibility, and enough calm in the interface to support experimentation without adding friction.

Core ideas

  • Prompt writing as a repeatable workflow, not a one-off action.
  • Versioning and comparison as first-class features.
  • Clear visual hierarchy for runs, results, and decisions.
  • A product design approach that keeps the experience calm, readable, and useful.

My role

Hoodi reflects my interest in product design, systems thinking, and the joy of turning abstract ideas into usable tools. I shaped the product direction, information structure, screen behavior, naming, and experience flow. The implementation was then brought to life with AI-assisted development.
